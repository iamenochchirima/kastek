# Parloa reference for the Labs landing page

Reviewed the live [Parloa homepage](https://www.parloa.com/) on 10 October 2026 with Chrome at 1440 × 1000. This is a visual study, not a specification for copying its assets or claims.

## Observed design

The homepage opens with a full viewport, warm human video. A light navigation sits directly over it. The white serif headline sits at the bottom left, with translucent conversation bubbles on the right and a small orange button below. It makes the customer's experience visible before explaining the platform. [Source](https://www.parloa.com/)

Chrome reports Season Serif for headings, Geist for body and navigation, and Geist Mono for small labels. At 1440px, the central content is 1184px wide with 128px side margins. Main headings are 54px with 56.7px line height and weight 440. White sections use warm ink #1F1C1B. [Source](https://www.parloa.com/)

Below the hero, a client logo strip leads into spacious editorial sections. Small labels precede large headings. Three visual panels have captions below them. A platform section pairs a left heading with right body copy, then a large diagram. Photographic testimonials, security proof, a warm orange closing panel and a structured white footer finish the page. [Source](https://www.parloa.com/)

## Adaptation for Labs

Lead with the saved headline, "Good ideas. Real working tools." The headline is already stronger than a long description of software categories. Place it over an original, warm photograph of a working business. Use a restrained dark overlay for readable text and a quiet navigation containing Products, Studio and Contact.

Give the photo a purpose. Pair it with a small example of an actual business task: a customer asks about an appointment, a response gets sent, and a follow-up reaches the team. Keep the example clearly illustrative. A full dashboard screenshot in the hero would compete with the photograph and dilute the first impression.

Replace the client logo strip with direct links to Agents, Growth and Agency. They are the actual product choices. Use the product names with one short description each, generous horizontal spacing and a thin divider. This gives the page the same quiet pause after the hero without inventing endorsement.

Keep the middle of the page mostly white. Use a large editorial heading to introduce the product family. Show the existing demos in broad, deliberate compositions with the product explanation beside them. Agents can show the inbox and handoff; Growth can show a lead and its follow-up; Agency can show a branded client workspace. Each example needs one visible action, rather than several tiny widgets that cannot be read.

Use contrasting scale, generous space and photographs to create visual interest. Avoid ornamental numbering, endless enclosing cards, generic gradient orbs and pseudo technical diagrams. A subtle reveal is enough; content should remain visible when animation is disabled.

Finish with a warm visual Studio invitation for custom software. This is a different buying path from choosing a product, so give it its own section and a concrete "Tell us what you're building" link. Keep the footer short.

## Implementation boundaries

Use an available serif with similar calm proportions, such as the existing serif or a properly licensed alternative. Season Serif's use on Parloa does not make its font files reusable. Preserve the Labs identity and saved tagline. Do not copy Parloa photography, marks, customer names, testimonials, certifications, result percentages or product terminology.

Individual viewport captures after scrolling revealed the animated panels. They combine small UI examples with orange, muted blue and dark visual backgrounds. The larger platform diagram has three white columns for building, testing and monitoring, above an infrastructure and integration strip. These are observable compositions, not features Labs should claim. [Source](https://www.parloa.com/)
