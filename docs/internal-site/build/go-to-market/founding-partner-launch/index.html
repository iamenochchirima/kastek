Founding Partner Launch Plan

Purpose​

Launch Kasitek with a small group of real businesses that use the product in their daily work, pay for an outcome-focused service, and help shape the product through observed use.

This is not a broad self-service launch. It is an assisted, repeatable launch programme that proves the customer operating system before it is scaled through self-serve onboarding or white-label partners.

Product Positioning​

Kasitek is a customer growth and operations workspace. It helps a business capture leads, respond on the channels customers use, qualify and follow up work, run campaigns, schedule content, and understand outcomes.

The initial offer is organised around outcomes rather than internal product names:

Capture a lead
  → keep the contact and consent record
  → respond or qualify through Inbox and agents
  → book, quote, follow up, or campaign to the contact
  → measure the outcome

Founding Partner Cohort​

Size and operating model​

  • Recruit 5–8 founding partner organisations.
  • Run each partner through a 90-day assisted programme.
  • Assign one Kasitek owner to each client account.
  • Hold a weekly operating review in the first month, then fortnightly once the workflow is stable.
  • Treat every deployment as both a paid service engagement and structured product research.

Ideal partner profile​

Prioritise businesses with a clear lead-to-outcome loop, meaningful inbound volume, and an owner who will review results with us:

  • clinics, medical aesthetics, wellness, dental, and health-adjacent services;
  • restaurants, franchise groups, hospitality, and accommodation;
  • airport transfers, tours, logistics, and transport services;
  • property, insurance, legal, accounting, and other professional services;
  • home services such as solar, security, repair, and installation;
  • agencies that can use the product across a small number of managed clients.

Avoid early partners that cannot identify a primary lead source, a measurable conversion event, or someone accountable for replying to escalated conversations.

Partner acceptance criteria​

Before onboarding, document:

  • the business’s primary customer journey;
  • existing lead sources and connected channels;
  • the target response-time and conversion problem;
  • one primary outcome: booked consultation, completed order, accepted quote, attended appointment, or sale;
  • the person who owns human follow-up;
  • consent and messaging policy requirements;
  • required provider access and channel ownership.

Initial Offer​

Lead Response​

For businesses that need to capture and answer inbound enquiries.

  • WhatsApp and web lead capture;
  • Contacts, tags, source, consent, and lifecycle stage;
  • unified Inbox and human follow-up;
  • a configured qualification or support agent;
  • response-time and lead-quality reporting.

Growth Engine​

For businesses that need repeatable follow-up and demand generation.

  • everything in Lead Response;
  • segments, WhatsApp campaigns, approved templates, and opt-outs;
  • follow-up tasks and campaign-to-Inbox handoff;
  • content calendar and connected social publishing where available;
  • campaign and conversion reporting.

Operations Partner​

For businesses with a defined booking, quote, or service workflow.

  • everything in Growth Engine;
  • form, quote, consultation, or booking flows;
  • reminders, reschedules, no-show follow-up, and workflow automation;
  • deeper agent configuration and managed optimisation;
  • monthly outcome review and a documented improvement backlog.

These are commercial packaging options. The platform must remain one shared workspace, customer record, activity timeline, and permission model.

  • Contacts is the user-facing relationship record. It includes leads, prospects, current customers, and past customers.
  • Pipeline is the only new primary launch destination. It owns lifecycle stages, assignments, tasks, follow-up, and outcomes.
  • Tasks, consent, notes, forms, bookings, and campaign participation belong in the Contact or Pipeline experience.
  • Calendar gains separate content and bookings views before Bookings becomes a standalone navigation item.
  • Channels appear inside Inbox only once they can create a contact identity, write to the customer timeline, enforce policy, and report delivery state.

Onboarding Journey​

Week 0: discovery and success design​

  1. Map the client’s lead-to-outcome workflow.
  2. Agree a primary use case and one measurable success metric.
  3. Confirm channel ownership, provider access, and consent approach.
  4. Choose the agent role, handoff owner, and escalation hours.
  5. Record the baseline: response time, lead volume, conversion, no-shows, or campaign performance.

Week 1: workspace and data foundation​

  1. Create the workspace, team access, and operational ownership.
  2. Connect WhatsApp and any available social accounts.
  3. Import contacts with source, tags, consent, and lifecycle data.
  4. Define pipeline stages, assignment rules, and overdue follow-up policy.
  5. Configure saved replies, templates, and initial reporting.

Week 2: live customer workflow​

  1. Activate the Inbox and lead-response process.
  2. Configure and test the first agent with explicit handoff rules.
  3. Route web leads, forms, or chat into Contacts and Pipeline.
  4. Train staff on the daily queue, assignments, notes, and follow-up.
  5. Run an observed first-day operating session.

Weeks 3–4: outcome loop​

  1. Launch the first approved campaign or nurture sequence.
  2. Add booking, quote, or service follow-up where relevant.
  3. Review agent handoffs, unanswered leads, and conversion quality.
  4. Fix workflow failures before expanding channel or feature scope.
  5. Produce the first owner summary and improvement plan.

Month 2 onward: expansion and proof​

  1. Add the second customer workflow only after the first is stable.
  2. Expand social, booking, campaigns, or additional channels as needed.
  3. Create a before-and-after case study using agreed metrics.
  4. Ask for a referral only after measurable value has been demonstrated.

Success Measures​

Every partner must have a shared scorecard. Measure both product adoption and business outcomes.

Activationworkspace configured, channel connected, contacts imported, first workflow live, first staff member active
Responsivenessfirst-response time, unassigned conversations, overdue follow-ups, human handoff time
Lead flowleads by source, qualification rate, pipeline movement, lost-reason trends
Conversionbooked consultations, quote acceptance, orders, attendance, repeat customer activity

Product Feedback System​

What to capture​

For each request or failure, record:

  • client and industry;
  • workflow and intended outcome;
  • affected contact, channel, or feature;
  • severity and frequency;
  • workaround used;
  • business impact;
  • whether it is a product gap, configuration gap, training gap, or provider limitation.

Prioritisation rule​

Prioritise work that:

  1. breaks the agreed customer workflow;
  2. appears across multiple founding partners;
  3. improves a measurable client outcome;
  4. strengthens a shared platform capability rather than a one-off customisation.

Do not add a new product area because one client asks for a labelled menu item. First identify the workflow, shared domain, and outcome it belongs to.

Sales Motion​

  1. Lead with a short workflow audit, not a feature tour.
  2. Demonstrate one industry-relevant lead-to-outcome flow.
  3. Propose a 90-day assisted outcome programme with a named scope.
  4. Charge setup or implementation where custom configuration is required.
  5. Charge a recurring workspace and managed-optimisation fee.
  6. Review outcomes monthly and convert successful deployments into documented case studies.

Launch Risks and Controls​

RiskControl
Too many disconnected featuresBuild only through shared Contacts, Pipeline, activity, consent, and outcome models.
Client expects fully autonomous AIDefine agent scope, handoff rules, approval boundaries, and escalation owner during onboarding.
Poor channel data or consentRequire source, consent, and opt-out data before campaign activation.
Provider/API limitationsShow only verified channel capabilities and keep unsupported channels frozen.
Custom work overwhelms product workRecord every request and promote only reusable workflow patterns.
Weak evidence of valueAgree a baseline and one outcome metric before activation.
